# Voiceover script — Probability reel

Two cuts of the same lesson. Record the shared blocks once; only Block H is
extra, and only Block 4's last line differs.

Pace target: **2.6 words per second**. Every scene length was derived from that
rate, so if you land inside the window without rushing, the animation will line
up on its own. Where a line says *(hold)*, stop talking and let the drawing
finish — the silence is doing the teaching.

Problem: a bag of 3 red and 2 blue marbles. Draw twice, without replacement.
P(one red and one blue) = 3/5.

---

## Cut A — straight (`probability-reel.mp4`, 2020 frames, 67.3s)

### Block 1 · Read the question — 0:00 → 0:17
> Here's the question, exactly how it shows up on a test.
>
> *(hold — let them see the whole sentence)*
>
> Three red marbles. Two blue. That's five in the bag.
>
> You want one red **and** one blue. You're drawing twice.
>
> And here's the phrase that changes everything: **without replacement**. The
> first marble does not go back.

### Block 2 · The bag shrinks — 0:16 → 0:30
> Watch what that does.
>
> You pull a marble out. It's gone. It's sitting over here now.
>
> So the bag doesn't have five anymore. It has four.
>
> Your second pick comes out of four, not five. That's the whole trick.

### Block 3 · Path 1, red then blue — 0:28 → 0:39
> There are two ways to get one of each. Let's do the first.
>
> Red first: three of the five are red. Three-fifths.
>
> Now that red one's gone. Four left, two of them blue. Two-fourths.
>
> Multiply: six-twentieths.

### Block 4 · Path 2, blue then red — 0:38 → 0:47
> But you could just as easily have grabbed blue first.
>
> Two of five are blue. Then three of the four left are red.
>
> Multiply: six-twentieths again.

### Block 5 · Add the paths — 0:46 → 0:58
> Both of those count. Either order gives you one red and one blue.
>
> So add them. Six-twentieths plus six-twentieths is twelve-twentieths.
>
> Divide top and bottom by four — three-fifths.

### Block 6 · The answer — 0:57 → 1:07
> Three-fifths.
>
> Two things to carry with you. **Without replacement** means the bottom number
> drops. And if there's more than one order, you count every order.
>
> Follow for more.

## Cut B — error-first (`probability-reel-hook.mp4`, 2214 frames, 73.8s)

Identical to Cut A, with **Block H** inserted after Block 1 and one extra line
at the end of Block 5. Everything shifts about 6.4 seconds later.

### Block H · The trap — 0:16 → 0:24
*(sits between Block 1 and Block 2)*
> Now here's where nearly everyone slips.
>
> Most people answer three-tenths.
>
> *(hold — let the cross draw)*
>
> That's only half the answer.

### Block 5 (Cut B) · Add the paths — 0:53 → 1:05
> Same as Cut A, then add:
>
> That three-tenths from earlier? That was Path 1 on its own. Three-fifths
> counts both.

Later blocks shift accordingly: Block 2 at 0:23, Block 3 at 0:35, Block 4 at
0:44, Block 6 at 1:03.

## Recording notes

- **Say "three-fifths", never "three over five".** Kids read fractions as words
  long before they read them as division.
- **Slow down on "without replacement" and on "four, not five."** Those are the
  only two moments where being misheard costs the whole lesson.
- Don't narrate the arithmetic while the pen is still drawing it. Name the
  result *after* the stroke lands.
- The banner across the top of every scene after the first is a reminder, not a
  line to read.
